The Journey

Where Ancient
Meets Tomorrow

Japan is unlike anywhere else on earth. It is the only place where you can stand beneath a thousand-year-old cedar torii gate at dawn, and be on a 320km/h bullet train through snow-capped mountains by noon.

This 10-day private journey takes you through five of Japan's most iconic destinations — each utterly different in character. We've curated a rhythm that balances major landmarks with slow, unhurried moments: a cup of matcha in a Zen garden, an evening stroll through Gion, a private onsen at a mountain ryokan under the stars.

Every hotel is hand-picked. Every guide is vetted. Every reservation — from the Shinkansen seat to the kaiseki dinner — is handled before you arrive.

01
Tokyo — Arrival
Welcome to Tokyo — The City That Never Sleeps
Land at Narita/Haneda · Private transfer · Shinjuku evening exploration
Transfer Shinjuku Evening

Your Japan journey begins as your flight descends over the endless ocean of city lights. Your private guide and driver will meet you at the arrivals hall — name board in hand — for a seamless transfer to your Shinjuku hotel, one of Tokyo's most electric neighbourhoods.

This Afternoon & Evening

  • Private airport transfer in an air-conditioned van, ~75 minutes depending on terminal
  • Hotel check-in and a cold welcome drink while your guide walks you through the next 10 days
  • Gentle evening walk through Kabukichō and Omoide Yokocho (Memory Lane) — tiny smoke-filled yakitori alleys that haven't changed since 1950
  • Optional: take the lift to the Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building's free observation deck for a first panoramic look over the city at night

02
Tokyo
Culture, Chaos & Cool — Meiji to Shibuya
Meiji Shrine · Harajuku · Shibuya Crossing · Shibuya Sky
Shrine Shopping Iconic View

Today takes you from a peaceful cedar forest to the most photographed intersection on the planet — and somehow it all makes sense in Tokyo.

Morning

  • Meiji Jingū Shrine — walk the long forested path in early morning quiet before the crowds arrive; this is Tokyo at its most contemplative
  • Nearby Yoyogi Park for a slow coffee and people-watching

Afternoon

  • Harajuku's Takeshita Street — Japan's pop-culture fashion epicentre, all pastel candy floss and decadent crêpes
  • Omotesando Avenue — the Champs-Élysées of Tokyo, lined with flagship architecture from Prada, Dior, and local designers
  • Shibuya Crossing — the busiest pedestrian crossing in the world. Stand in the middle during a green light. Feel the flow.

Evening

  • Shibuya Sky rooftop observation deck at sunset — 360° views from the top of the Scramble building, 229m above the crossing
  • Dinner in a traditional izakaya in Ebisu

03
Tokyo
Old Tokyo & Future Tokyo — Asakusa to TeamLab
Senso-ji Temple · Akihabara · TeamLab Planets immersive art
Temple Tech District Art Installation

A day of vivid contrasts — incense smoke at a 1,400-year-old temple in the morning, then walking barefoot through an infinity mirror room of ten million digital flowers in the evening.

Morning

  • Senso-ji Temple in Asakusa — Tokyo's oldest and most beloved temple, with the famous Kaminarimon Thunder Gate
  • Nakamise-dori shopping street for traditional souvenirs: ningyo-yaki cakes, folding fans, lucky charms
  • Rickshaw ride through old Asakusa lanes (optional)

Afternoon

  • Akihabara Electric Town — multi-storey electronics bazaars, anime figurines, retro arcades and maid cafés
  • Ueno Park stroll (cherry blossom season: spectacular)

Evening

  • TeamLab Planets — wade through knee-deep reflective water into vast rooms filled with drifting light, breathing flowers and infinite mirrors. One of the world's most extraordinary art spaces.

04
Hakone
Fuji-san & The Ryokan — Mountains, Onsen & Silence
Mt. Fuji views · Hakone Open-Air Museum · Private mountain ryokan · Outdoor onsen
Mt. Fuji Ryokan Onsen

Leave the city behind for the mountains. The drive southwest takes you into the Fuji-Hakone-Izu National Park — and on a clear day, the first glimpse of Fuji-san above the treeline stops the heart.

Morning

  • Depart Tokyo by private vehicle (approx. 90 minutes)
  • Kawaguchiko Lake viewpoint — arguably Japan's most iconic view: the reflection of Mt. Fuji in still lake water
  • Fuji Visitor Centre for historical and geological context

Afternoon

  • Hakone Open-Air Museum — 70,000 m² of sculpture gardens against a mountain backdrop, with works by Rodin, Picasso and Moore
  • Ropeway ride over the sulphurous Ōwakudani volcanic valley
  • Try the famous Kuro-Tamago — black eggs hard-boiled in volcanic spring water, said to add 7 years to your life

Evening

  • Check in to your private mountain ryokan — traditional inn with tatami floors, futon beds and a yukata robe laid out for you
  • Multi-course kaiseki dinner (Japanese haute cuisine) served in your private dining room
  • Late-night private outdoor onsen bath (rotemburo) under the stars

05
Hakone → Kyoto
The Bullet Train — 320km/h to Ancient Kyoto
Shinkansen experience · Arrive Kyoto · Gion evening walk
Shinkansen Gion Geisha District

The Tōkaidō Shinkansen is a journey worth taking slowly — or rather, at 320 km/h. Board at Odawara, and 75 minutes later you're in Kyoto, the city that refused to let go of its soul.

Morning

  • Ryokan breakfast — a beautiful spread of miso soup, pickles, grilled fish, rice and egg before departure
  • Transfer to Odawara Shinkansen station by private vehicle

Midday

  • Board the Hikari Shinkansen — reserved Green Car seats — to Kyoto
  • On a clear day: a breathtaking unobstructed view of Fuji-san from the right-side window between Shin-Fuji and Shizuoka stations
  • Arrive Kyoto station — the gateway to 17 UNESCO World Heritage Sites

Afternoon & Evening

  • Check into your Kyoto hotel near the Higashiyama district
  • Late afternoon: Gion neighbourhood stroll — cobblestoned Hanamikoji Street lined with ochaya (teahouses) and traditional machiya townhouses
  • If you're lucky (and patient), you may spot a maiko (apprentice geisha) in full kimono heading to an evening engagement
  • Dinner at a Pontocho alley restaurant perched over the Kamo River

06
Kyoto
10,000 Torii — Fushimi Inari at Dawn
Fushimi Inari early morning · Nijo Castle · Pontocho dining
Torii Gates Castle Dawn Hike

Set your alarm. This is the day you'll talk about for the rest of your life. The vermillion torii gates of Fushimi Inari at 6am — no crowds, just mist rising through the forest and the distant clang of a shrine bell — is one of travel's truly sacred experiences.

Early Morning

  • Depart hotel at 05:45 for Fushimi Inari Taisha Shrine — arrive before sunrise
  • Walk the lower loop (approx. 1 hour) through thousands of vermillion torii gates tunnelling up the mountainside
  • For the adventurous: continue to the summit (2–3 hours) for panoramic views over Kyoto and Osaka
  • Breakfast at a small teahouse at the shrine base on your return

Afternoon

  • Nijo Castle — the 17th-century shogun's Kyoto residence, with its famous "nightingale floors" (squeaky floorboards to detect intruders)
  • Nishiki Market — Kyoto's "Kitchen" — 400-year-old covered market with stalls of pickles, fresh tofu, grilled skewers and matcha everything

Meals

Breakfast — Shrine teahouse Lunch — Nishiki Market grazing Dinner — Yudofu (tofu hot pot) in Higashiyama

07
Kyoto
Bamboo, Gold & Zen — Arashiyama & Kinkakuji
Arashiyama bamboo grove · Tenryu-ji Zen garden · Golden Pavilion
Bamboo Grove Zen Garden UNESCO Site

Kyoto's western hills hold two of Japan's most arresting images — a bamboo forest where the stalks creak and sway in a cathedral hush, and a pavilion sheathed entirely in gold leaf that reflects into a mirror-still pond.

Morning

  • Arashiyama Bamboo Grove — enter the grove before 8am for the best light and fewest people; the hollow percussion of swaying bamboo is something the photographs simply cannot capture
  • Tenryu-ji — a 14th-century Zen Buddhist temple; its garden, framing the Arashiyama hills as a living painting, is considered one of Japan's finest
  • Monkey Park Iwatayama (optional) — hike for views and meet 120 free-roaming Japanese macaques

Afternoon

  • Kinkakuji (Temple of the Golden Pavilion) — the entire top two storeys are coated in 14-carat gold leaf; see it shimmer above its reflection pond
  • Ryoanji Temple — the most celebrated dry stone garden (karesansui) in Japan; fifteen rocks arranged in raked white gravel, the meaning of which is still debated after 500 years

Meals

Breakfast — Hotel Lunch — Matcha soba in Arashiyama Dinner — Shabu-shabu hot pot

08
Nara Day Trip
The Deer Park — Ancient Capital & Sacred Animals
Nara Park · 1,500-year-old Todai-ji Buddha · Freely roaming deer
Nara Deer Giant Buddha Day Trip

Japan's first permanent capital is now famous for something gloriously surreal: over 1,200 sika deer roam freely through Nara Park, considered sacred messengers of the gods. They bow back when you bow to them.

Morning

  • Express train from Kyoto to Nara (45 minutes)
  • Nara Park — enter the deer territory immediately from the station; buy shika senbei deer crackers and prepare to be surrounded and possibly head-butted
  • Todai-ji Temple — walk through the towering Nandaimon Gate (flanked by 8-metre wooden warrior guardians) to reach the world's largest wooden building

Afternoon

  • Inside Todai-ji: the Daibutsu — a 15-metre bronze Buddha, cast in 751 AD, still the world's largest bronze statue of Buddha
  • Pass through the wooden pillar with a hole in the base — said to be the size of the Buddha's nostril; those who crawl through receive enlightenment
  • Kasuga Taisha Shrine — a lantern-lined forest path to one of Japan's most atmospheric Shinto shrines
  • Return to Kyoto by late afternoon

Meals

Breakfast — Hotel Lunch — Kakinoha-zushi (persimmon-leaf sushi) Dinner — Kyoto kaiseki

09
Osaka
Osaka — Japan's Kitchen & Neon City
Osaka Castle · Dotonbori street food tour · Kuromon Ichiba market
Street Food Castle Night Market

Osakans have a saying: kuidaore — "eat until you drop." The city takes this seriously. It is the food capital of a country that is the food capital of the world.

Morning

  • 15-minute Shinkansen from Kyoto to Osaka (or scenic local train for the same price)
  • Osaka Castle and its surrounding parkland — the most visited castle in Japan; the tower houses a superb museum of the feudal Sengoku period
  • Kuromon Ichiba Market ("Osaka's Kitchen") — a bustling covered market with 180 stalls: fresh uni, wagyu skewers, whole octopus, towers of pickles

Afternoon

  • Shinsekai — a retro 1950s entertainment district that feels like a time capsule; try kushikatsu (deep-fried skewers) here, and nowhere else
  • Spa World (optional) — enormous multi-floor onsen complex themed after global bathing cultures

Evening

  • Dotonbori — Osaka's neon heartland; the giant crab arms spin, the Glico runner beams over the canal, and the smell of takoyaki (octopus balls) fills the air
  • Guided street food walk: takoyaki · okonomiyaki · kushikatsu · kakigori shaved ice · fresh oysters

Meals

Breakfast — Hotel Lunch — Kuromon Market Dinner — Dotonbori street food tour

Departure Transfer

Your last morning in Japan. There's no fixed programme — just time.

Morning

  • Final Japanese breakfast at the hotel — take it slowly
  • A last walk through Dotonbori if you want to pick up final gifts from the morning market stalls
  • Amerika-mura (American Village) for streetwear if you're a late shopper
